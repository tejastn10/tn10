I've finished this slim novella, written in Kannada by Vivek Shanbhag and translated by Srinath
Perur. Told by a young man in a middle-class family from Bangalore, it follows the household after
a spice business lifts them out of cramped poverty. The money arrives, the house gets bigger, and the
family's bonds tighten and curdle in ways nobody quite names out loud.

## Themes I Noticed

**Money and What It Does to Families**

- Wealth arriving suddenly and reshaping who holds power at home
- Old loyalties turning into quiet obligations and unspoken debts
- Comfort as something that has to be protected, at almost any cost

**Silence and Things Left Unsaid**

- A narrator who observes more than he acts
- Violence and ugliness suggested rather than shown
- The gap between how the family presents itself and what happens behind closed doors

**Entanglement**

- The title phrase, "ghachar ghochar", standing for something tangled beyond repair
- Family ties that can't be pulled apart without damaging the whole
- The narrator's drift into complicity as the price of staying comfortable

## Memorable Quotes

"Whatever fragrance the whole wardrobe had was missing in the individual clothes it held."

"In my thinking, what came to the family was mine. In her mind, my family and I were separate
entities."

"In any regular household, family members glare when a wife begins to spend her husband's
hard-earned money."

The book is short enough to finish in a sitting, but it lingers. Shanbhag keeps the prose plain and
the tone conversational, which makes the quiet turns of the story hit harder than a louder book
could. It's a tight, uneasy look at how comfort can cost a family more than hardship did.

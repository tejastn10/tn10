I've finished this graphic novel, written and illustrated by Joshy Benedict and translated from
Malayalam by K.K. Muralidharan. It follows Babycha, a gambling addict in rural Kerala who tries to
quit after marrying his beloved Paulikutty. Village men gather at midnight on an abandoned island to
play spot flip, and the story traces how he is drawn back in until he loses everything.

## Themes I Noticed

**Addiction and the Pull of the Game**

- Quitting as something that has to be re-attempted, not achieved once
- The thrill of the next hand outweighing everything Babycha has built
- How a vice can sit comfortably inside an ordinary, close-knit community

**Dark Humour, Dispassionate Voice**

- A narration that stays matter-of-fact even as the losses pile up
- Comedy and tragedy running side by side, with neither softening the other
- Letting the reader see the ruin coming before the character does

**Marriage and What's at Stake**

- Babycha's promise to build a life with Paulikutty as the thing he keeps gambling away
- The private island as a place outside everyday rules and consequences

Because the story is told in panels, the art does as much work as the text, and the village and its
midnight card games feel specific and lived-in rather than generic. It's an unflinching look at
addiction that still manages to be funny, which is a hard balance to hold. A good entry point for
Indian graphic novels.

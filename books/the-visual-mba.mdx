I've completed Jason Barron's sketch-based distillation of his MBA coursework. Drawn from his own
notes as a student, the book trades dense textbook prose for simple illustrations that make core
business concepts fast to absorb and easy to recall.

## Themes I Noticed

**Business Fundamentals**

- Reading the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement
- Accounting as "the language of business"
- Time value of money and why a dollar today beats a dollar tomorrow

**Entrepreneurial Innovation**

- Spotting customer pain points as the seed of any viable venture
- Validating an idea before scaling it
- Why most entrepreneurial wins come from solving an existing problem, not inventing a new one

**Leadership and Team Management**

- Leadership as distinct from management - inspiring change, not just directing tasks
- Motivating teams through the Motivating Potential Score
- Managing stress: helpful in small doses, corrosive past a certain point

**Strategic Decision-Making**

- Competitive positioning through Porter's Five Forces
- Branding as "what people think and feel when they see you"
- Ethical and cultural considerations in international expansion

## Memorable Quotes

"When people see you, what do they think/feel about you? That's your brand."

"Accounting is the language of business."

"Leadership is more than management. It's about inspiring change and improving results through who
you are and how you motivate others."

"Some stress is helpful to increase performance but there comes a point where performance drops.
Make sure to take breaks, exercise, and relax to maintain performance."

What makes this book stand out is the format itself - each concept gets a single hand-drawn page
rather than a chapter, so the ideas read more like a field guide than a textbook. It won't replace
a real MBA, but it's an efficient way to pick up the vocabulary and mental models business school
would otherwise take two years to teach, and a handy reference to flip back through later.
